# Camera-frame target experiment: first returned results

Run: `quick_fit_20260914_161014`. Three complete 1,000-update runs, 16 objects, 128 training observations and 64 held-out views of those SAME objects. Fixed training measurement: 32 views. Batch 4, seed 29, BF16, shape cross-attention + surface projector, 50% per-example training visual dropout. Both fixed measurements keep visual inputs present. Reported GPU: H200, Torch 2.5.1+cu121.

## Final fixed-bank losses

| Target / pointmap | Training reference | Held-out views |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Object / stock | 0.067066 | 0.062729 |
| Camera / stock | 0.125022 | 0.116813 |
| Camera / shared | 0.124385 | 0.118091 |

1. **Shared pointmap normalization provides no clear loss benefit in this test.** Camera/shared versus camera/stock differs by -0.000637 training (-0.51%) and +0.001278 held-out (+1.09%). Their curves nearly overlap throughout and the small differences change sign. This is the cleaner comparison: targets, surface input and all training settings match. No statistical equivalence claim: one seed, aggregate losses, no independent-sample confidence intervals.
2. **Camera targets did not make the recorded fitting objective easier within this budget.** Camera/stock final loss is about 86% higher on both training-reference and held-out measurements than object/stock. The difference already occurs on fitted views; it cannot be explained solely as failure on held-out views. However, changing target convention changes the latent distribution, so 86% is NOT a reconstruction-error ratio or proof that camera targets intrinsically cannot work.
3. **The camera runs have not established a hard floor.** Camera/stock improves 9.16% on the training reference and 7.16% on held views between updates 500 and 1,000, with small final fluctuations. More training could still change results; these curves give no positive reason to promote shared normalization or start a full run.

## Interpretation and next decision

Keep stock pointmap normalization as the default: its replacement has not helped this controlled loss comparison. Shared-input scale/translation alignment alone has not removed the camera-target fitting difficulty under this scope/dropout/budget. Do not generalize that statement into 'all coordinate effects ruled out.' Target/VecSetX units still differ, the target distribution changed, and fitting capacity/optimization remain possible factors.

Held-out loss being below training-reference loss is NOT evidence that view dependence is solved: these are different observations and different fixed noise/timestep banks. Same-object generalization is the only intended scope; no unseen-object claim is available. All arms retain real geometry, so these comparisons alone do not establish causal surface use.

The next measurement remains the previously planned final Stage-1 generated-reconstruction comparison, using the saved checkpoints and their own target conventions. Compare training and held-out views separately and reference decoded target occupancy. This is needed to connect the flow loss difference to actual shape prediction. Existing preparation output should supply target-decoder fidelity/figures; do not re-encode or launch another training run merely to obtain them. No new GPU script or job was created in this analysis.
